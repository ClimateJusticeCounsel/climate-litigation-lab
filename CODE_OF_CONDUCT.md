# Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

Climate Justice Counsel is committed to maintaining a professional, respectful, and constructive environment for contributors to the Climate Litigation Lab.

We welcome good-faith technical collaboration and rigorous discussion concerning the design, accessibility, security, and operation of the open-source software.

## Scope of Open-Source Participation

The Climate Litigation Lab makes portions of its software and technical infrastructure available as open source. This does **not** make the Lab's substantive, editorial, institutional, or event content open for public authorship or modification.

Unless expressly invited by Climate Justice Counsel, public contributions should be limited to matters such as:

- software and technical infrastructure;
- accessibility and usability improvements;
- bug fixes and security improvements;
- generic components and tools; and
- technical documentation concerning the software.

Climate Justice Counsel retains editorial control over event information, speaker information, programs and agendas, legal and research content, case materials, institutional statements, publications, and other substantive content.

Pull requests, issues, or other submissions seeking to add, modify, or remove such content may be closed without substantive review unless the contribution was specifically requested or authorized.

## Expected Conduct

Participants should:

- Communicate respectfully and constructively.
- Critique code, technical decisions, designs, and methodologies rather than individuals.
- Provide appropriate attribution for code, data, research, and other third-party materials.
- Respect intellectual property, privacy, confidentiality, and applicable professional obligations.
- Follow maintainers' instructions regarding the scope of contributions and project spaces.
- Correct technical errors transparently and respond constructively to good-faith feedback.

## Unacceptable Conduct

Unacceptable conduct includes:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, discriminatory conduct, or personal attacks.
- Deliberate disruption of project discussions or collaborative work.
- Publishing or threatening to publish another person's private information without authorization.
- Uploading, soliciting, discussing, or disclosing confidential, privileged, sensitive, or non-public information through public project spaces.
- Attempting to use issues, pull requests, discussions, or other project channels to solicit information concerning affected communities, litigants, speakers, researchers, partner institutions, or non-public Climate Justice Counsel activities.
- Plagiarism, deliberate misrepresentation of sources, or knowing submission of fabricated or materially misleading information.
- Impersonation or misleading claims of affiliation with Climate Justice Counsel or its partners.
- Repeated bad-faith participation after requests from maintainers to modify conduct.

## Content and Confidentiality

Contributors must not submit or disclose confidential client information, privileged material, protected personal data, non-public litigation strategy, unpublished research, internal correspondence, private event materials, or other information they are not authorized to make public.

The presence of public source code does not imply that Climate Justice Counsel's underlying research, data, records, event materials, or institutional content are public or licensed for reuse.

Published content appearing on the Climate Litigation Lab website remains subject to any copyright, attribution, or other notices applicable to that content.

## Project Governance

Climate Justice Counsel maintains and directs the Climate Litigation Lab and retains final authority over:

- the project's technical roadmap;
- acceptance or rejection of contributions;
- editorial and substantive content;
- event and institutional information; and
- the use of Climate Justice Counsel's name, logo, and branding.

Participation in the repository does not create an employment, agency, partnership, attorney-client, or other professional relationship with Climate Justice Counsel.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to official open-source project spaces, including GitHub issues, pull requests, code reviews, technical discussions, and other contributor channels maintained by Climate Justice Counsel.

Separate rules or participation terms may apply to academic workshops, conferences, research collaborations, or other Climate Justice Counsel activities.

## Enforcement

Project maintainers are responsible for interpreting and enforcing this Code of Conduct and may take proportionate action in response to conduct they determine to be inappropriate, harmful, or inconsistent with these standards.

Actions may include requesting changes in conduct, editing or removing contributions, closing discussions, restricting participation, or banning individuals from project spaces.

Enforcement decisions may take into account the seriousness of the conduct, whether it was repeated, and whether the participant responded constructively after concerns were raised.

## Reporting

Concerns about conduct may be reported privately to:

**[report@climatejusticecounsel.org](mailto:report@climatejusticecounsel.org)**

Reports will be reviewed as confidentially as reasonably possible. Information may be shared where necessary to investigate or address a report, comply with legal obligations, or protect participants.

Individuals responsible for handling a report should avoid participating in its resolution where they have a material conflict of interest.

## Attribution
